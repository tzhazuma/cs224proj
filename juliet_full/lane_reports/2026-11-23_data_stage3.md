All 3,236 units outside variant 12 get the same ASan/LSan verdict as in stage 1, in both layers. Two builds give the same SHA-256. **Construction** (seed 20261123)
- **Real frees:** every real free/delete in both labels sits under an always-true guard.
- **Flaw sites:** each one becomes a dead free of the sink pointer under an always-false guard. The form matches the allocation: `free` for C, `delete` after `new`, `delete[]` after `new[]`.
- **Extra dead frees:** 0 or 1 per group, drawn per group and placed at the same relative spot in bad and good. For realloc units the dead free goes right after the realloc and frees the new pointer.
- **Count matching:** free and guard counts are equal within every pair. Groups by free count: 720 with 1, 838 with 2, 98 with 3, 2 with 4.
- **Guards:** each is `static const int sym_N = V;` at the top of the file, using the six PLAN comparison forms. There are no globals.
- **Placebo:** the same insertion points and guards, but the dead statement is a `printLine(...)` and real frees are not wrapped.

**Deviations from §2.5** (decided on dev only; the numbers are dev BoW AUROC on B2G, holding out one variant at a time)
1. **Guards:** the PLAN form with a fixed value of 5 scored 0.989, because the comparison form alone gave away which free is real. Using values 4 or 5, so every form appears as both a true and a false guard, brought it to 0.605. Random guard names (sym_10 to sym_99) instead of sequential ones brought it to 0.572, which is the version I kept.
2. **G2B goods:** they have a flaw site too, so they get 1–2 dead frees; this is needed to match free counts between labels.
3. **No-flaw-site families:** realloc_fail and virtual_destructor have no flaw site, so both labels get 1–2 dead frees.
4. **Object deletes:** `delete baseObject` and similar are wrapped like ordinary frees.
