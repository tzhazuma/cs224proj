\begin{abstract}
Juliet is the standard synthetic test suite for memory-leak (\cwe) detectors, but its templates reveal the label.
On the original \cwe{} units, the rule ``\code{static void} $\Rightarrow$ good'' reaches macro-F1 \res{x}{probe_static_void_original},
and ``no \code{free}/\code{delete} $\Rightarrow$ leak'' reaches \res{grep-original}{macro},
above Clang SA (\res{clang_sa-original}{macro}), CodeQL and Infer.
We rebuild all 1,658 \cwe{} groups into a debiased benchmark of 3,316 compiled, sanitizer-checked units with a flow-variant split.
We add a stress layer with never-executed frees and a placebo layer with never-executed prints.
On it we compare four static analyzers, LineVul, two LLMs with four prompting strategies, two decision models, and a LoRA decision model fine-tuned on Juliet.
On the debiased layer, the best learned detectors reach \res{jev_L1-layer1}{macro}--\res{deepseek_L2-layer1}{macro}.
Dead frees separate the detectors.
Clang SA and Infer do not change.
The grep rule falls to \res{grep-layer3}{macro}.
Our fine-tuned model drops from \res{lora_L1-layer1}{macro} to \res{lora_L1-layer3}{macro} because it treats any \code{free} as a fix.
Small LLMs and Intern-Decision instead raise false alarms.
Letting the LLM extract facts and a Datalog rule decide loses the least among prompting strategies.
The placebo layer moves every detector much less, so the drops come from the dead frees and not from the edit itself.
\end{abstract}
